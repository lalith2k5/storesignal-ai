R5. Turning Store Chatter Into Action: The Voice-of-Store Engine 
Real-world pain: Head oﬃce is drowning in unstructured feedback  — customer reviews, app-
store comments, store -staﬀWhatsApp notes, complaint tickets. Real operational problems (a 
broken freezer in one store, a mislabelled allergen, a rude billing counter) are buried in nois e 
and surface weeks late, if ever. 
The problem: Convert a messy multi-source, multilingual feedback stream into a prioritised 
weekly action list, routed to the right owner. 
What we provide: 
 
 40k feedback records from four channels with genuinely diﬀerent writing styles: online 
reviews, in-app ratings with comments, store-staﬀshifl notes, and call-centre ticket 
transcripts 
 Code-mixed text (English, Hinglish, transliterated Tamil/Marathi) with typos, emojis, and 
truncation 
 Store master and a taxonomy of ~30 operational issue types with the owning function 
(Store Ops, Supply Chain, Merchandising, Digital, HR) 
 Hidden labels on a sample: issue type, severity, and whether it was "actionable" 
Assumptions students may make: 
 
 Feedback is unveriﬁed; a single angry review is not proof of a systemic issue 
 Volume diﬀers hugely by store size, so raw counts must be normalised 
 Anything mentioning safety, allergens, hygiene, or staﬀmisconduct is high-severity by 
policy, regardless of volume 
 No audio is provided (transcripts only) 
Desired output: 
 
1. A weekly one-page brief per region: top 5 issues, evidence quotes, aﬀected stores, trend 
arrow vs. last week 
2. Routing: each issue tagged with owning function and a severity level, with the escalation 
rule stated 
3. Emerging-issue detection — the killer feature: ﬂag an issue that is small in absolute volume 
but rising sharply at one store, before it becomes a national problem 
4. evaluate. py score = classiﬁcation quality on hidden labels + detection lead-time on three 
seeded "planted" incidents hidden in the data (this is where teams genuinely diﬀerentiate) 
5. A precision statement: of the issues you escalated, what fraction were truly actionable 
Stretch: a de-duplication layer so the same incident reported through four channels appears
