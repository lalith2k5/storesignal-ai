1. Executive Summary / Project Overview
1.1 Overview
StoreSignal AI is a proposed AI-assisted retail operations intelligence system that converts unstructured, multilingual
feedback into prioritized, evidence-backed actions for store and regional teams. It addresses the gap between
collecting customer and employee feedback and acting on emerging operational problems before they become
widespread or severe.
The project scenario contains approximately 40,000 feedback records from four channels:
  Online reviews
  In-app comments
  Staff shift notes
  Call-centre transcripts
 The records may contain code-mixed English and Hinglish, transliterated Tamil and Marathi, spelling errors, emojis,
abbreviations, incomplete sentences, and truncated text. The system is expected to map feedback to an issue
taxonomy of roughly 30 categories, identify severity and actionability, route incidents to the appropriate owning
function, and produce concise weekly regional briefs.
A central design principle is that risk and volume must be treated separately. Safety-related reports involving
potential allergens, food hygiene, or staff misconduct must be escalated according to explicit policy even when only a
small number of reports exist. Separately, the system should detect emerging issues that are increasing unusually
quickly within a store or region, even when their absolute volume remains small.
1.2 Proposed value
StoreSignal AI aims to help teams:
  Detect developing store issues earlier by combining signals across channels.
  Understand code-mixed and noisy feedback more reliably than a simple sentiment-only pipeline.
  Separate high-risk cases from ordinary high-volume complaints.
  Explain why an alert was generated and show the supporting source evidence.
  Assign a suggested owner and next action using the store master and issue taxonomy.
  Produce a concise weekly regional action brief.
  Measure classification quality, escalation precision, and early-warning lead time using the provided evaluation
 workflow.
1.3 Product position
Position StoreSignal AI as an early-warning and incident-resolution intelligence layer for retail operations, not
merely a sentiment-analysis dashboard. Its distinctive workflow is:
Raw feedback → normalized text → issue classification → cross-channel incident fusion → trend/risk
detection → evidence-backed alert → owner routing → action tracking → regional brief.
The proposed interactive dashboard is a demonstration UI using illustrative sample records. It is not yet evidence of a
working production model, and its simulated figures must not be presented as measured performance.

3. Detailed Solutions Discussed
Module A — Data ingestion and preparation
#### A1. Multi-channel ingestion Create a common record schema for online reviews, in-app comments, shift notes,
and call-centre transcripts. Preserve source channel, timestamp, store ID, and source-record ID when available.
#### A2. Data quality and normalization
  Handle missing fields, encoding issues, duplicate records, malformed timestamps, and empty text.
  Normalize whitespace and common text artifacts without erasing meaningful emphasis or negation.
  Preserve both raw text and normalized text for auditability.
  Treat emojis, abbreviations, typos, and truncation as signals rather than automatically deleting them.
  Apply privacy controls to personal information in transcripts and staff notes.
 #### A3. Code-mixed language intelligence Support English/Hinglish and transliterated Tamil/Marathi. Begin with
language-agnostic or multilingual text representations and add carefully designed normalization dictionaries for
common transliterations and retail terms. Avoid aggressive translation that can change meaning, severity, or negation.
Validation: Evaluate performance by channel and language pattern, not only on an overall average.
Module B — Issue classification and actionability
#### B1. Taxonomy-based classification Map each record to the relevant category or categories in the approximately
30-item issue taxonomy. Use the provided store master and taxonomy definitions as the source of truth for names and
owner mappings.
A practical baseline is a multilingual transformer or embedding-based classifier, compared against a simpler
TF-IDF/linear model. The final choice should be based on validation results, implementation time, and
reproducibility—not on model size alone.
#### B2. Multi-task prediction Where the dataset supports it, predict:
  Issue type
  Severity
  Actionability
  Owning function or routing category
 Keep the risk-policy layer separate from the statistical classifier so policy-required escalations cannot be suppressed
merely because a model score is low.
#### B3. Uncertainty handling Use confidence thresholds or calibrated probabilities only after validation. Send
ambiguous or low-confidence cases to human review instead of forcing a potentially incorrect category.

Module C — Early-Warning Radar
This is the signature capability for the hackathon demonstration.
#### C1. Detecting emerging issues Monitor counts or rates by store, issue category, and time window. Detect sharp
local increases even if the total count is not among the highest. Potential methods include rolling baselines, moving
averages, robust z-scores, or count-based anomaly methods; select the simplest method that performs well on the
available data.
#### C2. Store-size-aware intelligence Where exposure denominators are available—such as transactions, visits, or
units sold—compare rates rather than raw counts. If denominators are missing, use a documented proxy such as
historical store-specific volume and clearly label the limitation. Do not treat all stores as directly comparable when their
size or reporting volume differs.
#### C3. Cross-store pattern transfer Look for similar issue categories emerging at multiple stores in nearby time
windows. Where supplier, equipment, product, or region identifiers exist, use them as possible linking evidence. A
shared cause must remain a hypothesis until verified.
#### C4. Forecasting what may happen next As a stretch feature, estimate whether a trend may continue or worsen.
Start with transparent trend-based forecasts and clearly label them as estimates. Do not claim certainty or present
forecasts as confirmed incidents.
Module D — Cross-Channel Incident Fusion and Deduplication
#### D1. Incident fusion Group records that may refer to the same underlying incident using combinations of:
  Store and location identifiers
  Issue category
  Timestamp proximity
  Text similarity or multilingual embeddings
  Product, equipment, or supplier identifiers, if available
 #### D2. Deduplication Identify exact and near-duplicate messages so repeated copies do not artificially inflate issue
counts. Preserve links to the original records and avoid deleting source data.
#### D3. Root-cause connection graph Show related signals as a graph of reports, possible incident, store, time, and
relevant product/equipment entities. For example, a customer report about warm milk and a staff note about a noisy
freezer may be connected as a possible refrigeration incident.
The graph should explain why items were linked. It must distinguish a correlated pattern from a confirmed root
cause.
Module E — Safety-First Guardrail
Potential allergen, hygiene, food-safety, and staff-misconduct reports require policy-defined high-priority handling
regardless of volume.
Implementation principles:
  Maintain an explicit list of mandatory-risk categories and routing rules.
  Apply rules independently of emerging-volume detection.
  Make policy matches visible in the alert explanation.
  Route to the appropriate safety, compliance, or people function defined by the project taxonomy.
  Use human review and established operational procedures for consequential decisions.
  Do not let deduplication or a low classifier score silently remove a risk signal.
 The system should support both risk escalation and trend detection as separate mechanisms. A single severe
report may require immediate review even when no trend exists.

Module F — Evidence-Backed Alerts and Explainability
Every alert should provide:
  A concise summary of the suspected issue
  Supporting text snippets and source channels
  Store, time window, and issue category
  Number of unique reports and any deduplication notes
  Why it was flagged: policy rule, rising trend, cross-channel evidence, or a combination
  Confidence or uncertainty, if validated
  Suggested owner and next action
  A link or reference to the underlying records
 Avoid unsupported causal language. Use wording such as “possible common refrigeration issue” until evidence
confirms the cause.
Module G — Automatic Action Routing
Use the issue taxonomy and store master to map alerts to an owning function. Create a routing table with:
  Issue category
  Default owner
  Required severity
  Mandatory escalation rule
  Suggested first action
  Escalation destination or SLA, if supplied by the project
 Routing should be deterministic for mandatory policy rules and configurable for normal operational categories. A
human should be able to correct the owner and record the reason.
Module H — Regional Brief Generator
Generate a concise, one-page weekly brief for regional managers. It should prioritize action over narrative.
Recommended structure:
  Executive summary: most important changes this week.
  Priority incidents: severity, store, evidence, owner, and next step.
  Emerging signals: sharp increases, small-but-rising issues, and cross-store patterns.
  Safety and compliance: policy-triggered items requiring review.
  Actions due: owner, status, and overdue items where tracked.
  Data caveats: missing denominators, low-confidence classifications, or limited evidence.
 The language model, if used for summarization, should summarize only retrieved evidence and structured findings. It
must not invent counts, causes, or actions.
Module I — Incident Replay Simulator
The replay feature is a strong demonstration device. It shows the timeline of an incident as records arrive:
  First weak signal
  Additional report from another channel
  Trend or fusion trigger
  Alert and severity decision
  Owner routing and recommended action

For the three seeded incidents, replay should demonstrate when the system would have detected the issue and how
much lead time it achieved relative to the evaluation reference point. Do not hardcode the seeded answers or use
hidden labels to tune the detector.
Module J — Action Impact Simulator
As a stretch feature, allow a manager to compare response options, such as inspection, maintenance escalation, or
following a product-safety procedure. Show urgency, owner, effort, and the rationale for each option. Any estimated
benefit must be clearly labeled as a scenario estimate, not a measured result.
Module K — Human Feedback Learning Loop
Let reviewers label alerts as correct, incorrect, duplicate, already resolved, or misrouted. Keep an audit trail. Use
feedback for error analysis and future model improvements; do not automatically retrain on unreviewed or noisy
feedback.
Module L — Incident-to-Resolution Tracker
Track the workflow: Detected → Reviewed → Assigned → Action taken → Resolved
Capture timestamps, owner, action note, resolution status, and repeat incidents where feasible. This turns StoreSignal
from a reporting tool into an operational follow-through tool.
Module M — Trust, Evaluation, and Monitoring
Track:
  Classification quality by issue category
  Macro-F1 or other metrics appropriate for class imbalance
  Precision and recall for actionable alerts
  Precision of escalated actionable issues
  Detection lead time on the three seeded incidents
  False alerts per store or per week
  Duplicate/fusion quality
  Coverage and error rates by channel and language pattern
  Policy compliance for mandatory-risk cases
  Human override and resolution outcomes
 A trust panel should show real evaluation values only after running the actual evaluation. Until then, use blank
placeholders or clearly marked illustrative demo values.
Module N — Interactive Demo Prototype
The discussed StoreSignal AI UI includes:
  Command Center: headline indicators, priority queue, and recent feedback stream
  Early-Warning Radar: selectable incident scenarios and chronological replay
  Incident Explorer: search, evidence, owner, severity, action, and replay
  Regional Action Brief: region selector and exportable text brief
  Model & Trust: evaluation placeholders and validation checklist
 The current UI is a prototype using simulated data. It is useful for storytelling and user-flow validation but is not
connected to the 40,000-record dataset or a live model. Before judging, label all simulated data clearly and avoid
presenting placeholder metrics as achieved results.

5. Next Steps & Recommendations
5.1 Recommended final product definition
StoreSignal AI is a multilingual retail operations early-warning system that fuses customer and staff feedback,
detects emerging and high-risk incidents, explains alerts with source evidence, routes actions to the correct
teams, and tracks follow-through.
5.2 What to prioritize for selection
  Prove one end-to-end scenario. A reliable flow is more convincing than many disconnected features.
  Make early detection measurable. Report lead time for the three seeded incidents and show the replay timeline.
  Demonstrate safety policy explicitly. A low-volume allergen, hygiene, or misconduct signal should still trigger the
 policy-defined escalation.

  Begin with a simple, reproducible baseline before adding complex models.
  Keep statistical predictions, trend detection, and policy escalation as distinct components.
  Use time-aware evaluation for incident detection and avoid data leakage from duplicate or future records.
  Use exposure-normalized rates when credible denominators exist; otherwise document the proxy and its limitations.
  Preserve raw source records and provenance so every alert is auditable.
  Keep a human in the loop for consequential safety, compliance, and staff-related decisions.
  Generate summaries only from structured, evidence-backed findings.
  Do not use hidden evaluation labels to train, tune, or hardcode seeded incidents.
  Measure error rates across channels and language patterns to identify uneven performance.

5.6 Final recommendation
Build the smallest version that can reliably demonstrate the complete chain: multilingual classification,
emerging-issue detection, mandatory-risk escalation, evidence-backed routing, and a regional brief. Add the
root-cause graph and action simulator only after that chain works.
The winning message is not “we use AI on feedback.” It is: “We detect weak signals across store channels,
explain why they matter, escalate risk regardless of volume, and help teams act before the problem spreads.”

Appendix B — Terminology
  Actionability: Whether a record indicates a practical issue that merits a response.
  Emerging issue: An issue whose recent rate or pattern is unusual relative to an appropriate baseline.
  Incident fusion: Grouping records that may refer to the same underlying operational event.
  Lead time: How long before a reference point the system first detects a seeded incident, according to the evaluation
 definition.
 Mandatory-risk escalation: Policy-driven escalation for designated safety or conduct categories regardless of
 volume.
 Store master: Reference data linking store IDs to attributes such as region and other available operational metadata.
  Evidence-backed alert: An alert whose explanation points to the source records and measurable signals that
 triggered it.
